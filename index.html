The problem

An approval is only true until something changes.

A model update, a new tool or an edited prompt can quietly undo last quarter's sign-off. Most firms find out when an auditor asks.

Evidence is scattered

Security tests, model evaluations, privacy reviews and sign-offs sit in different tools and slide packs.

Approvals expire silently

The approval was for the version that was checked. Nobody is told when that is no longer the version running.

Your system, not the vendor's model

A model card says nothing about how your prompts, tools, data and permissions behave together.

How it works

From inventory to approval, in six steps.

  1. 01

    Authorise

    Assessment starts with written permission and an agreed scope. No permission, no checks.

  2. 02

    Inventory

    Record each AI system: its model, prompts, tools and data, and the version of each.

  3. 03

    Check inside your boundary

    A signed runner inside your network runs the checks. Your credentials never leave it.

  4. 04

    Keep the evidence

    Every result is written to a record that cannot be changed afterwards.

  5. 05

    Review and fix

    People set the severity, then record the fix or accept the risk by name.

  6. 06

    Approve, and know when to re-check

    The approver gets one evidence pack. When the system changes, the approval is marked out of date.

How it is designed

Built for someone entitled to doubt you.

NexTrust is designed around four ideas.

Your credentials stay with you

Checks run inside your own boundary and only the results come out. We never hold access to your AI systems.

A missing result is never a pass

If a check could not run, the control shows as not evaluated, and the evidence pack says so.

Every control cites its source

Each control names the standard, the version and the provision it comes from, and says what it does not check.

Evidence that cannot be quietly changed

Records are only ever added, and each is chained to the one before, so an altered or missing record is detected.

Checked against the standard you are held to. This preview uses ETSI EN 304 223, the European Standard that grew out of the UK AI Cyber Security Code of Practice. In an engagement the standard is the one your firm chooses, and every control cites its version. A mapping to a standard supports your judgement; it is not a compliance determination. ETSI is named for reference; NexTrust is not affiliated with or endorsed by it.

Limits

What NexTrust does not do.

An assurance product is only worth having if it is honest about its limits. We put these in writing at the start of every engagement.

  • No certificationNexTrust does not certify anything and holds no accreditation to do so.
  • No compliance verdictControl mappings support your judgement. They are not a compliance determination.
  • Your decisionWhether to deploy stays with your accountable people. NexTrust presents the evidence.
  • Permission firstNo assessment without written authorisation from the system owner and an agreed scope.
  • Not production by defaultNo checks against production systems or data without explicit, scoped approval.
  • No attack testingNexTrust checks that controls are in place. It does not attack your system, and the pack says where a security test is still needed.
